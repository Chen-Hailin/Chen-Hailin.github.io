What I Know About
Large Language Models
Compiled from research, experiments, and practical experience.
Last updated: May 2026.

I. The Transformer Architecture

At their core, LLMs are next-token predictors trained on vast corpora of text. The transformer architecture — specifically the decoder-only variant — uses stacked self-attention layers and feed-forward networks. Attention mechanisms allow each token to "look at" every previous token, building contextual representations through learned query-key-value projections.

The key insight: scale unlocks emergent abilities that smaller models simply don't exhibit. These aren't explicitly programmed — they arise from the statistics of language at scale. Instruction following, in-context learning, chain-of-thought reasoning — none were designed into the architecture, yet they emerge beyond certain compute thresholds.

◆ ◆ ◆

II. Training Pipeline

  • Pre-training — Autoregressive language modeling on trillions of tokens. This is where the model acquires linguistic knowledge and world facts. Cost: millions in compute.
  • SFT — Supervised fine-tuning on human-written demonstrations. Teaches the model to follow instructions and produce useful responses.
  • RLHF / DPO — Reinforcement learning from human feedback or direct preference optimization. Aligns model outputs with human values and preferences.
  • Continual pre-training — Domain-adaptive training on specialized corpora (code, math, scientific papers) to improve specific capabilities.

A less discussed fact: the data mix matters more than architecture at this point. Most LLMs use nearly identical transformer stacks. The differentiation comes from data curation, filtering, deduplication, and the precise ratios of different data sources.

◆ ◆ ◆

III. Inference & Reasoning

LLMs reason through auto-regressive decoding — generating one token at a time conditioned on all previous tokens. This process is fundamentally sequential and computationally expensive. Key techniques:

  • Chain-of-Thought — Intermediate reasoning steps before the final answer. Dramatically improves performance on math, logic, and multi-step tasks.
  • Scaffolding / Agents — LLMs augmented with tools (code execution, search, APIs). The model plans, executes actions, observes results, and iterates.
  • Speculative Decoding — Using a draft model to propose tokens, then verifying with the target model. 2-3x speedup without quality loss.
  • KV-Cache — Caching key-value projections from previous tokens. Essential for efficient generation but memory-intensive for long contexts.
◆ ◆ ◆

IV. My Research Perspective

My work has focused on three axes of LLM capability:

  • Modularity & Structure — Breaking generation into reusable sub-modules (CodeChain). Structured outputs for evaluating reasoning (StructTest). Label modular prompts for controllable text classification.
  • Distillation & Efficiency — Personalized distillation adapts large models to specific code generation tasks while keeping the student small and local. The question of whether open-source models can catch up to proprietary ones.
  • Reliability & Trust — RAG for factual grounding, contamination detection in evaluation benchmarks, and faithful generation. Before we trust LLMs with critical decisions, we need to understand their failure modes.

The open question that drives me: Can we make LLMs reason like humans — not just pattern-match, but truly generalize? The answer likely involves architectures beyond pure next-token prediction, combined with more sophisticated training signals than next-token likelihood.

◆ ◆ ◆

V. What I'm Watching

  • Long-context models — 1M+ token contexts (Gemini, Claude) change what's possible. The limit shifts from "can the model remember" to "can the model find the signal in the noise."
  • Test-time compute scaling — o1-like models that spend more compute at inference time for harder problems. Reasoning as a budget you can allocate.
  • Multi-modal grounding — Connecting language to vision, audio, code execution. The next frontier is agents that interact with the real world.
  • Small models — 1-8B parameter models that run on-device. The race isn't just about bigger — it's about smarter per parameter.
